WHAT IS SOLID ENOUGH TO PUT ON SCREEN

Intake is the strongest thing we have and it is not a demo trick. The wizard
runs in four steps: Upload, Contract Details, Contacts and Fees, Verification.
Behind it there is a real resolution stage that reads a whole packet and works
out which document actually governs, so four counter offers and an amendment
resolve to one set of controlling terms instead of the last file read winning.
Every extracted value carries a citation back to the document name and page,
and there is a second verification pass over the critical fields that flags
disagreement rather than hiding it. That is the part competitors describe and
do not show.

Dates are computed, not typed. The dependency engine derives every task date
from two anchors, the contract acceptance date and the closing date, using
finish-to-start and start-to-start links with day offsets, including offsets
that read values out of the contract such as the inspection period. That is why
a deal lands with a full dated task list a few seconds after creation.

The AI genuinely sends some mail by itself. Five rows in the task library are
marked Automated, the welcome emails, the title order and the documentation
review, and the executor completes them without anyone clicking. The safety
model around it is real and worth saying out loud in a pitch: those bodies are
fixed library templates rather than free written by a model, recipients can
only come from parties captured off the contract, the deal has to be active, it
has to have a connected mailbox, and anything overdue past a threshold is
surfaced to a human instead of firing a stale email at a client. Everything
else still waits for a person.

The tree itself is healthy today. Backend suite is 1388 passing and the
frontend typechecks clean.

WHAT I WOULD NOT SHOW, AND THE CORRECTION

I told you the inbound email side was rebuilt. It was, and then it was reverted

removed a surface that also carried outbound drafts. So the code that is
